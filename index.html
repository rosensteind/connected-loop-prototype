Everyone around one pupil, seeing the same picture.

A boy is seen in a week by his housemaster, his tutor, three subject teachers, a clinician and a coach. Each holds a fragment. None of them holds the whole, and his parents — who often know most — sit outside the loop entirely.

Walk through it — 5 minutes What happens to what you write

Nothing to install and no sign-in. Everything in the walkthrough is fictional.

The thing no one person can see

Four low check-ins a boy told nobody about. An engagement drop only one teacher saw. A phone call home only his mother heard. Individually, none of it warrants a phone call. Together, inside six days, it is the whole argument.

  1. 1

    He checks in, in thirty seconds

    Three questions chosen with him, not assigned to him. His streak, his wins, his words. Framed as his own instrument, because a boy who experiences it as monitoring stops answering honestly.

  2. 2

    The adults contribute what they already notice

    A teacher adds one observation a week. A housemaster logs a conversation. A parent sends an early signal from home. Each takes under a minute, and none of them has to chase anybody.

  3. 3

    A clinician decides how far clinical news travels

    A discharge or a change in treatment reaches the people managing his day, at a level the clinician sets: coordinator only, the pastoral team, or the family as well. Raw clinical detail never leaves the clinical circle.

  4. 4

    Converging signals surface for a person to act on

    When independent sources agree inside one week, it is raised. It prompts a conversation. It never decides anything, sends anything, or scores him.

Who it is for

Six roles, each with a genuinely different view of the same record. What you see is what your job needs, and no more.

The pupil

His own check-in, streak and wins. A route to a person, above everything else on the page.

Teachers

One page on him, a morning flag, and one box after the lesson. Never a pastoral note, never a clinical one.

Pastoral leads

The full picture, and the pattern view that puts every source on one time base.

Clinicians and therapists

Their own record, and the handoff to the school that they control. Compartmented from each other.

Parents

A weekly digest in, an early signal out. Their own child's record and nothing else.

Coordinator

The pastoral loop, and the requests that need a person today. Not the clinical record.

What this is not

Not a diagnostic tool. Nothing here identifies, screens for, or rules out any condition.

Not a treatment. It delivers no therapy, no intervention, and no clinical advice.

Not monitoring. It does not watch, score, or rank a child.

Not an emergency service. Nobody is reading it at three in the morning, and it says so on the page where that matters.

That boundary is regulatory as well as clinical. Software intended to diagnose, monitor or treat can be a medical device under the UK Medical Devices Regulations 2002. This sits outside that regime deliberately. Read the full position.

Where it stands

A working prototype with fictional pupils. Before a real child's name goes anywhere near it, two things have to be true, and neither is a formality.

A written safeguarding response path

Who answers when a pupil asks for someone, within what time, and who covers evenings and weekends. A child must not have a "find me" button before somebody has agreed who comes.

A signed-off Data Protection Impact Assessment

Mandatory under Article 35 for special-category data about children. A pilot of six is not a way around it.
